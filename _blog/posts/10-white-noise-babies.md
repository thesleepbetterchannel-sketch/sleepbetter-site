For many tired parents, a sound machine feels like a lifesaver. Steady sound can soothe a fussy baby and cover the noise of a busy house. Used carefully, it's a reasonable tool. Used carelessly, it can be louder than you'd ever guess.

## What the research found

In 2014, a team at The Hospital for Sick Children in Toronto published a study in *Pediatrics*, the journal of the American Academy of Pediatrics. They tested [14 infant sleep machines at full volume](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2013-3617), measuring the sound at 30 cm (about a foot), 1 meter and 2 meters away, adjusted for the size of a baby's ear canal.

What they found:

- **Every machine** was louder than 50 decibels at 30 cm, which is the recommended noise limit for hospital nurseries.
- **Three machines** went above 85 decibels at that distance. Played that loud for more than 8 hours, that exceeds the workplace noise limit for adults.
- **Even 2 meters away** (about 6½ feet), [all but one machine still topped 50 decibels](https://enttoday.org/?p=9218) at full volume.

The researchers concluded that these machines are capable of producing levels that could harm infant hearing and auditory development.

## What the study didn't show

It's important to read this calmly. The study measured **maximum** volume, not how parents actually use the machines. As NPR's report on the study noted, [it didn't look at real-world volume or distance, and it didn't show that any baby's hearing had been damaged](https://www.kunc.org/npr-news/2014-03-02/noise-machines-to-help-babies-sleep-can-raise-quite-a-din). What it shows is that the volume dial matters a lot.

## How to use sleep sounds safely with a baby

The study's authors gave [clear advice for families](https://hearingreview.com/hearing-loss/patient-care/pediatric-care/new-study-finds-infant-sleep-machines-can-contribute-hearing-loss-babies):

1. **Place the device as far from your baby as you can.** Across the room is better than on the dresser beside the crib.
2. **Never put it in the crib or on the crib rail.**
3. **Keep the volume low.**
4. **Run it for shorter stretches** rather than at full blast all night. A timer helps.

A simple check: stand where your baby's head will be. If the sound is more than a gentle background hum, turn it down.

## Keep the crib clear

Sound machines and phones belong outside the sleep space. The American Academy of Pediatrics' safe sleep guidance is that babies should sleep on their backs on a [firm, flat surface with a fitted sheet and no other bedding or soft objects](https://safesleep.mo.gov/?p=1265). That includes pillows, toys, bumpers and loose blankets, and it applies to gadgets too. Don't tuck a speaker or phone in beside your baby.

## Do you need a sound machine at all?

Not necessarily. Many babies sleep perfectly well with ordinary household sound. If you do use one, think of it as a gentle background layer, not something to drown out the world. And if you have any questions about your baby's sleep or hearing, your pediatrician is the best person to ask.
