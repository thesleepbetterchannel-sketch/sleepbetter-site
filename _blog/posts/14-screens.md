You've probably heard that phones ruin sleep. The real picture is more specific, and more useful, than that. It isn't the screen itself so much as the light it gives off, and when you're exposed to it.

## The study everyone cites

In a [2015 study published in *PNAS*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25535358/), researchers at Brigham and Women's Hospital had 12 adults read for four hours before bed on five nights in a row, either on an iPad or from a printed book, in a carefully controlled lab.

After reading on the tablet, people:

- [took longer to fall asleep and felt less sleepy in the evening](https://neurosciencenews.com/ebook-light-circadian-rhythm-1652/),
- had lower levels of melatonin, the hormone that rises at night to help bring on sleep,
- had body clocks that shifted later by more than an hour,
- spent less time in REM sleep,
- and were groggier and slower to wake up the next morning, even after eight hours in bed.

The lead researcher said the [most surprising finding was how tired people felt the next morning](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/light-emitting-e-readers-detrimentally-shift-circadian-clock).

## Why light matters so much at night

Your body clock uses light as its main signal for what time it is. In the evening, as light fades, your brain starts releasing melatonin. Bright light, especially the blue-rich light from LED screens, tells your brain it's still daytime. That holds back melatonin and nudges your clock later, so you feel alert at bedtime and sluggish in the morning.

## Keeping it in perspective

Four hours of reading on a bright tablet in a lab is more than most people do. Some researchers argued that [real-life light exposure is messier than the lab](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4386345/), for example because daytime light also affects how sensitive you are at night. The authors disagreed, and the debate is a good reminder that one study isn't the whole story.

The overall direction is clear, though: bright light late in the evening works against sleep. How much it affects you depends on how bright, how close and how long.

## Practical ways to cut back

You don't have to give up your phone. A few changes go a long way:

- **Set a screens-down time** 30 to 60 minutes before bed. Switch to a paper book, a podcast, music or a sleep sound.
- **Dim everything in the evening.** Turn screen brightness right down and use warmer, dimmer lamps around the house.
- **Use night mode if you like,** but don't count on it alone. Turning the brightness down and putting the phone away earlier are surer bets.
- **Keep the phone out of reach,** or at least across the room. The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about_sleep/sleep_hygiene.html) recommends keeping TVs, computers and phones out of the bedroom altogether.
- **If you play sleep sounds from your phone,** start the track, turn the screen off and put it face down.
