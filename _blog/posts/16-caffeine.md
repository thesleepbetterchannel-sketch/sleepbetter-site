If you sleep badly but "caffeine doesn't affect me," this one is for you. One of the clearest studies on caffeine and sleep found that it can cost you a surprising amount of sleep without you ever realizing.

## The study

Researchers at the Henry Ford Sleep Disorders and Research Center and Wayne State University gave [12 healthy sleepers 400 mg of caffeine](https://sleepeducation.org/late-afternoon-and-early-evening-caffeine-can-disrupt-sleep/), about two to three cups of coffee, at bedtime, three hours before bed, or six hours before bed, plus a night with no caffeine at all. Each person also took dummy pills at the other times, so nobody knew when the real dose came. Their sleep was tracked at home with a sleep monitor and a diary.

The results:

- **Caffeine at every one of those times disrupted sleep,** including six hours before bed.
- **Six hours before bed, it still cut measured sleep by more than an hour.**
- **People didn't notice.** Their sleep diaries suggested they were [largely unaware of the disruption](https://aasm.org/late-afternoon-and-early-evening-caffeine-can-disrupt-sleep-at-night/).

The lead researcher put it simply: a big cup of coffee on the drive home from work can affect your sleep much like a cup closer to bedtime. Based on the results, the authors suggested that [caffeine is generally best avoided after 5 p.m.](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/11/131114155838.htm) for healthy sleep.

## What that means for you

400 mg is a lot to take in one go, and it was a small study, so the exact numbers won't apply to everyone. People also differ in how quickly they clear caffeine. But the main lesson holds: **caffeine lasts much longer than the buzz,** and you may not be the best judge of how it's affecting you.

## A simple rule of thumb

- **Set a cut-off at least six hours before bed.** If you go to bed at 10:30 p.m., that's about 4:30 p.m. Earlier is better if you're sensitive or sleep badly.
- **Count all the sources.** Tea, cola, energy drinks, pre-workout powders, some pain relievers and chocolate all contain caffeine.
- **Watch the size.** A large coffee-shop coffee can hold far more caffeine than a home-brewed cup.
- **Try decaf in the afternoon.** It keeps the ritual of a warm drink without most of the caffeine.

## Run your own test

Because people often can't feel caffeine's effect on their sleep, the best way to find out is to experiment. For two weeks, stop caffeine by early afternoon and keep everything else the same. Note how long it takes you to fall asleep, how often you wake, and how you feel in the morning. Many people are surprised.

## Don't swap one problem for another

If you rely on caffeine to get through the day, it's often a sign you aren't getting enough sleep to begin with. Cutting back on afternoon caffeine while protecting your bedtime usually works better than trying to power through.
