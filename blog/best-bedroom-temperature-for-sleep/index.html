The Best Bedroom Temperature for Sleep

Sleep specialists usually recommend a cool room, around 60 to 67°F. Here's why your body likes it cooler at night, and what to do if you can't control the thermostat.

November 6, 2026 · 3 min read

Most of us keep our bedrooms warmer than sleep experts would like. If you often wake up hot, kick the covers off, or toss and turn on summer nights, temperature may be part of the problem.

The number to aim for

Sleep specialists at Cleveland Clinic recommend keeping your bedroom at about 60 to 67°F (15 to 19°C). They suggest thinking of your bedroom as a cave: cool, dark and quiet. Above about 70°F, they say, a room is too warm for good sleep. Below about 60°F, it's too cold.

That's a range, not a rule. Bedding, sleepwear and your own body all make a difference. But if your thermostat sits at 72°F overnight, try turning it down a few degrees.

Why cooler helps

Your body temperature follows a daily rhythm. In the evening your core temperature starts to fall, and that drop is part of how your body moves into sleep. A review of research on temperature and sleep by Japanese sleep scientists found that being too hot or too cold both tend to increase wakefulness and reduce deep sleep and REM sleep. Humid heat is especially disruptive, because it makes it harder for your body to shed heat.

The same review points out that bedding and clothing matter as much as the room. What counts is the little climate between you and your covers. A cool room with a warm duvet can feel perfect. A cool room with no blanket can feel miserable.

If you run hot

  • Lower the thermostat a few degrees an hour or so before bed.
  • Use a fan. Moving air helps your skin shed heat, and the steady hum doubles as a sleep sound.
  • Choose breathable bedding. Cotton or linen sheets and a lighter blanket for warm months.
  • Go easy on caffeine and sugary snacks in the evening. A Cleveland Clinic sleep specialist notes that both can raise your body temperature and make it harder to sleep comfortably.

If you run cold

  • Warm your feet. Socks can help you feel comfortable enough to drift off.
  • Layer your bedding so you can add or remove a layer without fully waking.
  • Keep the room cool and the bed warm, rather than heating the whole room.

Sharing a bed with someone who disagrees

This is one of the most common bedroom arguments. Rather than compromising on a warm room, keep it on the cooler side and let the warmer sleeper add layers. Separate blankets help a lot. In many countries, two single duvets on one bed is completely normal.

Babies

Infants are different. Cleveland Clinic suggests a slightly warmer range for babies and toddlers, 65 to 70°F, and overheating should be avoided. Dress them in breathable layers and check the back of the neck: if it's sweaty, remove a layer.
