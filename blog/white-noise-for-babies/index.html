White Noise for Babies: What Pediatric Research Says About Volume and Distance

Sound machines are a staple in many nurseries, but a study in Pediatrics found some can get surprisingly loud. Here's how to use sleep sounds for your baby safely.

November 6, 2026 · 3 min read

For many tired parents, a sound machine feels like a lifesaver. Steady sound can soothe a fussy baby and cover the noise of a busy house. Used carefully, it's a reasonable tool. Used carelessly, it can be louder than you'd ever guess.

What the research found

In 2014, a team at The Hospital for Sick Children in Toronto published a study in Pediatrics, the journal of the American Academy of Pediatrics. They tested 14 infant sleep machines at full volume, measuring the sound at 30 cm (about a foot), 1 meter and 2 meters away, adjusted for the size of a baby's ear canal.

What they found:

  • Every machine was louder than 50 decibels at 30 cm, which is the recommended noise limit for hospital nurseries.
  • Three machines went above 85 decibels at that distance. Played that loud for more than 8 hours, that exceeds the workplace noise limit for adults.
  • Even 2 meters away (about 6½ feet), all but one machine still topped 50 decibels at full volume.

The researchers concluded that these machines are capable of producing levels that could harm infant hearing and auditory development.

What the study didn't show

It's important to read this calmly. The study measured maximum volume, not how parents actually use the machines. As NPR's report on the study noted, it didn't look at real-world volume or distance, and it didn't show that any baby's hearing had been damaged. What it shows is that the volume dial matters a lot.

How to use sleep sounds safely with a baby

The study's authors gave clear advice for families:

  1. Place the device as far from your baby as you can. Across the room is better than on the dresser beside the crib.
  2. Never put it in the crib or on the crib rail.
  3. Keep the volume low.
  4. Run it for shorter stretches rather than at full blast all night. A timer helps.

A simple check: stand where your baby's head will be. If the sound is more than a gentle background hum, turn it down.

Keep the crib clear

Sound machines and phones belong outside the sleep space. The American Academy of Pediatrics' safe sleep guidance is that babies should sleep on their backs on a firm, flat surface with a fitted sheet and no other bedding or soft objects. That includes pillows, toys, bumpers and loose blankets, and it applies to gadgets too. Don't tuck a speaker or phone in beside your baby.

Do you need a sound machine at all?

Not necessarily. Many babies sleep perfectly well with ordinary household sound. If you do use one, think of it as a gentle background layer, not something to drown out the world. And if you have any questions about your baby's sleep or hearing, your pediatrician is the best person to ask.
